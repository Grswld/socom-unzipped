## 1. How it was played

`npx tsx tools/playtest.ts` (a dev server on 5192) opens `?map=MP2&redotcom`, `?map=MP6&redotcom` and
`?map=MP72&redotcom` in headless Chromium **on the host's GPU** (ANGLE on D3D11, an RTX 4070 SUPER: the frame times
are real, 16.7 ms at rest), clicks the canvas (the gesture the audio waits for) and enters walk. The input is the
owner's: the keys held with Playwright's keyboard in real time, and a **pad** -- a fake `navigator.getGamepads()` that
the page polls like a real one, so its bytes go through `./gamepad`, the look law and the move stick exactly as a
DualShock's would. Each map's spawn A, and the one of eight headings 2.5 s of the run gets furthest along ("open").

Per map: the run at pad bytes 64, 32 and 0 (research 79's light levels and full); the strafe right, the diagonal and
the back run at full stick; a full right-stick turn in place; Triangle tapped (crouch), a crouch walk at byte 64,
Triangle held (prone), a crawl, Triangle held (stand); the standing jump, then the running jump off a 0.9 s run; R1
held 0.7 s in each fire mode (L3 cycles them); R (reload); d-pad up twice, down twice (the zoom); 4 and R1 held
0.7 s (a grenade) and its explosion. On Frostfire also: the walk-off at the 142 deck, the sniper ladder to the 160
deck, the crate climb (X), a jump-grab at the crate, and the peek right (E) and left (Q).

Every frame of every scenario is sampled through `window.__viewer` (feet, eye, posed root, clip, blend, traversal,
stance, airborne, view) and scored: frames over 50 ms (a screenshot's own stall excluded), the largest camera move
that is not the feet's (teleports excluded), the root's largest step, a clip change with no cross-fade, an idle clip
in the air or under a moving body; the audio's counters before and after (events, sounds played by name, drops). The
speeds read off frames carry one 60 Hz tick of quantisation in a 0.4 s window (+-4 %): the exact speeds are the feel
table's (research 88), and within that the three maps agree with it everywhere.

## 5. Round 4 (2026-09-29): issue 6 read in the engine -- faithful, and closed

**What the engine does when its camera is pulled in close: nothing more than the pass.** Read in
`game/analysis/socom2_game.elf.decomp.c`:

- **No fade or hide by the camera's distance.** The actor's fade is `FUN_00552780(target alpha, rate, actor,
  priority)` (decomp 418720): `actor+0x230` the alpha it goes to, `+0x234` the rate, `+0x238` a priority, the model's
  alpha at `(actor+0x28)+0x9c`. It has exactly two callers, neither the camera's: the online respawn fade-in (1.0 at
  4 a second, `FUN_00599b60` 455695) and an online death fade-out (0 at 0.1 a second, `FUN_005979a0` 454491). The pass's
  distance `DAT_003de268` has no reader outside `FUN_0029bf70` (143391-143445) and its initialiser; `FUN_00296f10`

- **No near-plane change.** The projection's near plane is the map's `m_near_plane` (reCOM `zCamera/zcam.h:78`), 4.0 on
  every map, and the viewer already takes it (`fly.setClipPlanes(map.camera?.nearPlane ?? 4, ...)`); nothing in the
  camera's path writes it.
- **No further clamp.** The pass pulls the eye in at once to 0.75 short of the hit and keeps it at least 0.75 from the
  target; the main probe re-finds the wall every frame, which renews the 1.5 s hold, so against a wall the eye stays in

**So the frame the playtest showed is the console's framing, and the first reading of it was wrong.** Re-measured at the
same spot (`setCamera({x: 796, y: 115.4, z: 614, yaw: 270})`, D for 1 s): a wall 7.4 behind the look-at target holds

### 9.1 A tooling correction first: every earlier playtest drew with WebGL2

Playwright's bundled headless shell offers a WebGPU adapter but fails the device (`Failed to create device:
DynamicLib.Open: dxil.dll Windows Error: 87`), and three falls back ("WebGPU is not available, running under WebGL2
backend"). `tools/playtest.ts` launched that shell, so rounds 1-5 above measured **WebGL2**, not WebGPU. The installed
Chrome (and Playwright's full `chromium` channel) creates the device. `playtest.ts` now takes `--channel` (default
`chrome`) and records the backend each map drew with; `release-sweep.ts` asks for each backend explicitly (WebGL2 by
hiding `navigator.gpu`) and checks that `stats().backend` came back as asked. It did in all 74 runs.
